Kaze Emanuar techniques on Super Mario 64 (partial reference)

ILYAAS KAPADIA · 2026-11-18

Update 2026-11-22: numbers in this post predate the corrected console model (clock units, overlapped units, bus contention) and the log-scaled reward. Current figures: GCC -O2 1.38×, r9-kaze-partial 1.44×. The full human reference is now r10-kaze-master (1.73× public / 1.69× across all 25 routes).

Before running 24-hour autonomous agents on Super Mario 64, we wanted to answer a basic benchmark question: how much real-hardware speedup is actually achievable under strict gameplay, video, audio, and 4 MB RDRAM gates by an engineer who knows the Nintendo 64 inside out?

On the US retail cartridge, Nintendo's core game engine was compiled with SGI IDO at -O0. Simply flipping Makefile to mips64-elf-gcc -O2 -fno-fast-math (r1-gcc-o2) already yields a 1.18× console speedup (+0.155 reward). If a benchmark saturates at compiler flags, it is not testing systems engineering. Over the past five years, Kaze Emanuar has published dozens of dissections of the VR4300 CPU, its direct-mapped caches, Fast3D RSP microcode, and the 62.5 MHz Reality Display Processor — plus SM64-but-some-optimizations-are-removed. We audited 26 of his N64 optimization videos and engineered a progressive 3-rung hardware reference ladder (r7, r8, and r9) using the exact same compiler, profiler, and 12-course grading harness given to agents.

Rung 1 (r7-kaze-icache-trig): the 0xFC000 I-cache collision & 1-ULP math

Inspecting build/us/sm64.us.map reveals an immediate pathology on the VR4300's 16 KB direct-mapped instruction cache (512 lines × 32 bytes, indexed by vaddr[13:5]):

  • rendering_graph_node.o(.text) begins near 0x8027C800 (vaddr & 0x3FFF = 0x0800).
  • SEG_ENGINE (surface_collision.o(.text)) is pinned at 0x80378800 (vaddr & 0x3FFF = 0x0800).
  • Their separation is 0xFC000 = 63 × 16,384 bytes — an exact multiple of the 16 KB I-cache.

Every time the scene-graph renderer casts an object shadow, shadow.c calls find_floor in surface_collision.o, which maps to the exact same cache lines as rendering_graph_node.o and evicts the scene-graph walker mid-traversal. Worse, cold 6.5 KB audio/heap.o (boot/level-init allocator tables) sits right inside the hot per-frame audio synthesis loop. Repacking sm64.ld so rendering_graph_node.o, math_util.o, graph_node.o, and shadow.o sit contiguously inside one 16 KB window (and compiling cold menu, debug, crash-screen, and Goddard title TUs with -Os) cuts thousands of I-cache fills per frame.

Next are SM64's lookup tables: gSineTable + gCosineTable (20 KB) and gArctanTable (2 KB) exceed the entire 8 KB direct-mapped D-cache. Every random angle lookup evicts a 16-byte line of live object or matrix state. Replacing sins/coss exposed two subtle precision traps:

  1. Decal Z-fighting at 22 ULPs: a 3-term polynomial (max_err ≈ 2.6e-6) shifts wall/floor decals on Whomp's Fortress frame 19 just enough to flip depth comparisons on 0.45% of pixels. Upgrading to a 4-term minimax Chebyshev polynomial brings maximum error down to 1 ULP (5.96e-8) while keeping zero table reads.
  2. The atan2s (angle >> 4) bucket cliff: SM64's sins(x) quantizes 16-bit angles by shifting right 4 bits (steps of 16, or 0.088°). Even a ±1 LSB error in atan2_lookup flips the 4-bit bucket whenever an angle lands on a 0x...0 boundary — jumping Lakitu's camera focus by ~1.9 world units at distance 1,200! Adding two 128-byte residual bitmasks (256 bytes total vs 2,050 bytes) makes atan2_lookup 100% bit-exact across all 1,025 octant entries while shrinking the D-cache footprint 8×.

Combined with a 6-cycle IEEE-754 mantissa random_float() (0x3F800000U | (rnd << 7)), alias-safe stackless mtxf_mul, and 32-bit packed mtxf_to_mtx, r7 passes all 12 courses with Infinity dB audio SNR.

Rung 2 (r8-kaze-collision-objects): 3-line struct Surface, Bowser's Sub & Hyrum's Law

Vanilla struct Surface is 48 bytes (three 16-byte VR4300 D-cache lines), laid out so lowerY/upperY sit at 0x06..0x09 while vertex coordinates straddle 0x0A..0x1B across two cache lines. Every triangle rejected by find_floor_from_list or find_ceil_from_list pulls two or three cache lines from RDRAM. In r8, we repack struct Surface on 16-byte alignment:

struct Surface {
    /* Line 0 (0x00..0x0F): 2D XZ triangle & Y bounds — single-line rejection */
    TerrainData lowerY, upperY, v1x, v1z, v2x, v2z, v3x, v3z;
    /* Line 1 (0x10..0x1F): plane equation (fetched only when XZ + Y pass) */
    struct { f32 x, y, z; } normal;
    f32 originOffset;
    /* Line 2 (0x20..0x2F): wall Y coords & metadata */
    TerrainData v1y, v2y, v3y, type, force;
    s8 flags; RoomData room;
    struct Object *object;
} __attribute__((aligned(16)));

Over 90% of non-overlapping triangles now reject on Cache Line 0 alone. On top of that, vanilla SM64 zeroes the entire 12 KB gDynamicSurfacePartition array every frame and re-transforms every collision triangle of every platform object — even giant stationary meshes like Bowser's Submarine in Dire Dire Docks Area 2 and the Sunken Ship in Jolly Roger Bay, computing a fresh cross-product and sqrtf per triangle 30 times a second. Tracking dirty partition cells via a 256-bit mask and caching transformed surfaces for stationary platforms eliminates thousands of redundant transforms per second. Together with single-find_floor shadow caching and squared-distance hitbox checks, r8 jumps to 1.22× console speedup.

Catching Hyrum's Law in the water courses

While building r8, we also replaced clear_objects()'s eager 240-slot free-list walk with a lazy bump watermark (gObjectPoolWatermark), leaving gFreeObjectList.next = NULL until an object is unloaded. All eight land courses passed with Infinity dB audio SNR — and all four water courses (jrb, ddd, jrb2, ddd2) failed both video and audio at the exact frame Mario entered the water!

Comparing obs.bin frame-by-frame pinpointed the culprit in seconds: every non-RNG state field was bit-identical, while rng diverged on ACT_WATER_PLUNGE. Grepping gFreeObjectList across the codebase revealed exactly three callers outside the allocator — bubble.inc.c:15, water_objs.inc.c:77, and try_to_spawn_object() in object_helpers.c:546 — all three 1996 water-splash and bubble helpers peeking if (gFreeObjectList.next != NULL) directly to ask whether the pool had spare capacity. Because the bump allocator hadn't recycled an object yet, SM64 thought the 240-object pool was full and suppressed every water splash sprite and bubble sound effect. Updating those three checks to object_pool_has_free_slot() restored bit-exact RNG and Infinity dB audio across all 12 courses.

Rung 3 (r9-kaze-partial): lazy Mtx, CACHE 0x0d, RDP batching & the fillrate wall

Vanilla geo_process_object computes a full 3×3 rotation matrix, multiplies it by the camera matrix, allocates a 64-byte fixed-point Mtx on the display-list pool, and converts 16 floats to Q16.16 fixed-point before calling obj_is_in_view() — throwing all of that work away for every off-screen actor, and allocating dead Mtx structs for invisible parent nodes that never append a display list. In r9:

  • Translation-first frustum cull + lazy Mtx: geo_process_object computes only the 3-float camera-space translation first, runs obj_is_in_view immediately, and defers alloc_display_list(sizeof(Mtx)) + mtxf_to_mtx until geo_append_display_list actually emits geometry.
  • CACHE 0x0d (Create Dirty Exclusive): newly allocated 16-byte-aligned display-list buffers and 64-byte Mtx blocks claim dirty D-cache lines directly without wasting RDRAM bus reads on stale bytes about to be overwritten.
  • RDP & RDRAM bandwidth: switching opaque render modes to Kaze's G_RM_RA_ZB_OPA_SURF eliminates per-pixel IM_RD framebuffer reads while preserving Video Interface edge anti-aliasing; grouping identical spinning-coin display lists in LAYER_ALPHA shares TMEM uploads and drops redundant gsDPPipeSync stalls; off-screen 3×3 skybox tiles are culled before gDPLoadBlock; and double-buffered 16bpp framebuffers + exact _engineSegmentNoload BSS clearing reclaim ~304 KB of RDRAM (leaving 153.6 KB of contiguous headroom below the 4 MB ceiling).

Across the 12-course suite, r9-kaze-partial wins on all six hardware profiles simultaneously — cutting console frame time to 1.23× over the 1996 retail ROM, cutting 62.5 MHz RDP busy cycles by 13.3% (1.15×), cutting total RDRAM bus traffic by 15.5% (1.18×), and shrinking live RDRAM footprint by 8.8%.

What remains above r9 for 24-hour agents?

Looking at the per-course hardware counters shows why SM64 is such a rich 24-hour benchmark even past r9: Jolly Roger Bay draws massive translucent water quads and distance fog over the entire screen, burning ~18 ms on the 62.5 MHz RDP alone alongside 4.3M RSP Fast3D microcode cycles, while Bob-omb Battlefield and Rainbow Ride remain dominated by VR4300 + Fast3D vertex/display-list throughput. Pushing further toward locked 30 FPS on 4 MB retail silicon invites deeper architectural work — static display-list vertex cache optimization, Fast3D RSP microcode tuning (clipping/lighting vectorization in rspF3DBootStart), per-room octree/BVH surface indexing, and ABI1 audio command compaction — all verifiable automatically by the grader.
