Mario Eval v4: Held-Out Generalization, Hardware Docs, and Retiring v3 Scores

ILYAAS KAPADIA · 2026-11-27

A benchmark is only as honest as what happens when a frontier coding agent spends 24 uninterrupted hours pushing against every seam in its environment. After auditing multi-hundred-step agent transcripts from v2 and v3.1 line by line — alongside design lessons from Mechanize's GBA Eval — we are releasing Mario Eval v4 and resetting the public leaderboard so every published number comes from the hardened v4 task.

What we saw in 24-hour frontier runs

Across v2 and v3.1, agents discovered genuine Nintendo 64 systems engineering: switching from 1996 SGI IDO -O0 to GCC -O2/-O3, enabling Nintendo's F3DEX2 RSP microcode, claiming scratch matrices with VR4300 CACHE 0x0d (Create Dirty Exclusive), replacing sins/coss LUTs, and shrinking struct Surface to reduce 8 KB direct-mapped D-cache misses. At the same time, pouring over the raw transcripts exposed four places where our v3 environment measured eval-specific workarounds or rewarded overfitting instead of pure N64 engineering:

  • Public-course overfitting (40% public weight). Because the 6 public development courses (bob, wf, jrb, ddd, bitfs, rr) carried 40% of the final score and left temporary obs.bin traces in /tmp, an agent could write if (gCurrLevelNum == LEVEL_JRB) branches directly inside the C renderer or tune visibility tables around the exact camera coordinates of the 240-frame public clips.
  • Boot-table spoilers in mev.c. Our harness boot selector originally hardcoded 11 course IDs in a C switch statement inside src/game/mev.c, inadvertently telling any agent that read the file which courses existed in the held-out pool and preventing agents from booting the remaining courses to test their own changes.
  • An Early-Z tracker bug in our RDP cost model. In ares's instrumented RDP model, our shadow Z-buffer tracker reset when a fill-rectangle cleared colorAddr == zAddr. Pointing Set_Color_Image at a scratch address or changing Z-buffer addresses could desynchronize the shadow tracker from actual Z clears and undercount pixel writes.
  • 4 MB RDRAM wrapping under disabled Expansion Pak. With the 8 MB Expansion Pak disabled in ares, physical addresses above 0x00400000 ignored or wrapped before reaching the 4 MB RDRAM footprint bitmap, meaning an accidental high-address probe could go uncounted unless explicitly trapped on the bus.
  • Homemade cost-model prose instead of primary N64 hardware specs. Handing agents a custom prose walkthrough of our grader's internal equations mixed benchmark contract rules with optimization hints, whereas real systems engineers work from hardware architecture manuals and profiler counters.

What changed in Mario Eval v4

  1. Single-target Super Mario 64 benchmark. We removed the synthetic Pip's Garden calibration toy completely. Every line of the repo, harness, and leaderboard now targets the real 1996 Super Mario 64 codebase on retail 4 MB N64 hardware.
  2. 0% public weight, 100% unseen held-out scoring across 18 courses. The 6 public courses remain available inside the container via mev check so agents have a fast, deterministic verification loop, and every submission must still pass all 6 public courses plus PLAY=1 retail console cold-boot. However, public courses now carry 0% weight in the score: 100% of the score comes from 20 unseen host-only held-out trajectories (75% scripted multi-course and multi-act routes, 25% seeded random-input routes spanning 18 SM64 courses and sub-areas). Temporary obs.bin, video.bin, and audio.bin artifacts are unlinked immediately after gate verification.
  3. Universal level/act boot + differential mev diff. mev_select_boot_level() in src/game/mev.c now decodes any valid SM64 (level, act) pair digitally on frame 0 with zero course-list spoilers, and the container includes baseline.z64 plus mev diff <replay.txt> and differential mev stress (with symbolized VR4300 fault PCs) so agents can build whole-ROM tests on any stage they choose.
  4. Vendored N64brew hardware specs + compact zero-spoiler TASK.md. We replaced our homemade cost-model guide with 20 SHA-256-verified community N64 hardware reference pages from the N64brew Wiki (CC BY-SA 4.0) covering the VR4300 pipeline, MIPS III / COP1 FPU instructions, 16 KB / 8 KB caches, RDRAM bus timing, RSP vector unit, RDP rasterizer pipeline/hazards, and VI/AI/PI/SI/MI DMA interfaces, paired with a 26-line MEASUREMENT.md and a 48-line read-only TASK.md.
  5. Hardened RDP Early-Z, OOB RDRAM, and DMA coherence gates. The RDP shadow-Z tracker now tracks exact Z-buffer physical addresses, Set_Prim_Depth, and !zCompare && zUpdate writes; physical RDRAM accesses in 0x00400000..0x03EFFFFF are counted and gated at zero; DP_DMA checks every 16-byte cache line without early exit; and static integrity checks enforce structural parity between benchmark and PLAY=1 builds.
  6. Uncapped N64 FPS on the hero chart + faster C++ devloop. We keep log-scaled RL reward (log2(speedup) / 4) for scale-invariant optimization signal, while making Uncapped N64 Console FPS (starting from the ~34 FPS 1996 baseline against the N64's 60 Hz refresh line) the primary homepage chart view alongside Speedup (×), vs GCC -O2, and Bounded Cost Saved (%). Moving MIPS ELF word scanning and 4,224-byte gameplay observation matching into -O3 C++ and running PLAY=1 concurrently with course workers cuts mev check turnaround roughly in half.

Why prior v2/v3 model runs are retired

Because v4 changes the held-out route suite, sets public-course weight to 0%, closes the Early-Z and OOB RDRAM blind spots, and replaces the in-container task prompt and documentation, comparing v2/v3 trajectories directly against v4 runs would mix two different benchmarks. All pre-v4 runs remain preserved in git history, while the live v4 leaderboard starts fresh on the 26-route v4 suite alongside our recalibrated reference ladder (1996 IDO baseline, GCC -O2, Kaze Master Reference, and Beyond Kaze).
