Calibrating the grader

2026-11-16

A reward function for 24-hour RL or agent evaluation has to get every case you already understand right before you trust it on open-ended search. Before running agents, we built 16 calibration patches on Pip's Garden and 15 calibration patches on Super Mario 64 (spanning retail IDO, GCC -O2, individual Kaze Emanuar techniques, our full-stack partial Kaze reference, and deliberate reward-hacking traps) and graded every one across public and held-out routes. Full tables live on the calibration page.

Every shortcut fails, and the grader names why

Eleven negative patches across the two games cut work in ways a player would notice or that violate evaluation integrity. Every one scores −1.0 across all six profiles:

  • -ffast-math (both libdragon's default on Pip's Garden and GCC -O2 -ffast-math on SM64) is the most natural first flag an agent tries, and both fail gameplay verification when non-IEEE float contractions drift simulation state.
  • Halving physics substeps (2 substeps instead of 4 in Pip's Garden, or 2 quarter-steps instead of 4 in SM64's mario_step.c) is caught immediately on frame 1 by gameplay verification.
  • Muting audio synthesis fails audio SNR (0 dB against the 40 dB floor).
  • Dropping particles or forcing low-poly Mario/Pip passes gameplay checks and is caught cleanly by the video gate (failing 0.54%–4.8% of pixels against the 0.20% limit).
  • Touching mev.c or mev.h is rejected by SHA-256 integrity checks before build.

Whole-frame accepted visual variants

Strict pixel equality against the 1996 ROM would penalize two optimizations that N64 engineers universally consider equal-or-better visual quality: Kaze Emanuar's full-detail Mario LOD fix (always drawing high-poly Mario instead of popping to medium/low-poly meshes at 600/1600 units) and SGI's official G_RM_RA_ZB_OPA_SURF reduced-aliasing opaque render mode (preserving VI edge coverage anti-aliasing while dropping unnecessary per-pixel IM_RD framebuffer reads on opaque surfaces). We precompute those variants from tiny declarative patches, verify at precompute time that they leave gameplay and audio untouched, and check each candidate frame as a whole frame against either the baseline picture or one accepted variant — preventing per-pixel cherry-picking while rewarding genuine N64 rendering upgrades.

Cross-budgets catch unprincipled resource trades

Lookup tables trade RAM for CPU cycles; aggressive unrolling trades I-cache traffic for instruction count. On Pip's Garden, precomputing terrain heights (r2-engine) and adding a 16 KB sine LUT (r6-sine-table) win on speed while failing the zero-regression memory profile (−1*), whereas deleting an unused 256 KiB buffer (r4-memory) or double-buffering SM64's framebuffers (r5-double-buffer, saving 150 KiB of the 4 MB RDRAM budget) scores cleanly on memory.

The direct-mapped cache lottery

On Pip's Garden, two ways of forcing full-detail LOD differed by 96 bytes of code size; one shifted globals into an 8 KB direct-mapped D-cache collision with the stack and ran 17.8% slower despite executing fewer instructions. On Super Mario 64, the opposite happened when we inspected sm64.us.map and moved math_util.o adjacent to rendering_graph_node.o in sm64.ld: VR4300 I-cache RDRAM traffic dropped by over 50% on Bob-omb Battlefield. Reliable N64 optimization requires owning linker layout rather than hoping the compiler gets lucky.
