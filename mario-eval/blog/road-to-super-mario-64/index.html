Wiring Super Mario 64 into Mario Eval

2026-11-17

Mario Eval was designed from day one for Super Mario 64: a launch-window 1996 N64 codebase famously shipped at IDO -O0 in its core game engine, studied for decades by speedrunners and decompilers, and pushed to its hardware limits by Kaze Emanuar. Wiring n64decomp/sm64 into a leak-proof 24-hour RLVR benchmark required solving five engineering constraints cleanly.

1. Zero Nintendo code or assets in the repo

sm64/setup.sh takes a legally dumped US .z64 ROM you supply locally, verifies its SHA-1 (9bef1128717f958171a4afac3ed78ee2bb4e86ce), clones n64decomp/sm64 at pinned commit 9921382a into git-ignored sm64/decomp/, extracts assets inside Docker, and immediately deletes baserom.us.z64. When building mario-eval-task, Docker build assertions verify that zero .z64 ROMs, zero held-out routes, and zero calibration patches enter the agent image.

2. Unthrottled deterministic frames + PLAY=1 console gate

In benchmark builds (#ifndef PLAY), thread5_game_loop reads controller inputs synchronously per logical frame (mev_read_pad_sync()), waits for both MESG_SP_COMPLETE and MESG_DP_COMPLETE on each M_GFXTASK, steps two deterministic 544-sample M_AUDTASK subframes (1088 stereo samples per 30 Hz frame) through rspAspMain, and emits @mev over IS-Viewer. Every submission must also pass make check-play (PLAY=1), which restores normal 60 Hz VBlank pacing and asynchronous thread4_sound console execution so agents cannot rip out real-hardware scheduling.

3. Twelve courses booted without savestates

Emulator savestates pin one build's RAM layout and break as soon as an agent repacks structs or relocates BSS. Instead, our harness boots a clean initialized Save File A and routes frame-0 controller chord inputs directly into 12 courses (6 public: Bob-omb Battlefield, Whomp's Fortress, Jolly Roger Bay, Dire Dire Docks Area 2 / Bowser's Sub, Bowser in the Fire Sea, and Rainbow Ride; 6 private held-out: Cool Cool Mountain, Lethal Lava Land, Shifting Sand Land, plus alternate trajectories bob2, jrb2, and ddd2).

4. Player Equivalence & Hyrum's Law

Requiring 0-ULP internal struct equality on SM64 would ban Kaze's best optimizations (hibernating distant off-screen objects, replacing 22.5 KB of trig LUTs with minimax polynomials, or reordering scene-graph culling). Conversely, allowing arbitrary engine rewrites would break 1996 SM64 physics and glitches (quarter-steps, wall-kick windows, BLJs). Our SM64 gameplay gate enforces Player Equivalence: exact level/area, ±1-frame (and exact final) stars/coins/health and Mario action transitions, and tight sub-pixel L-infinity tolerances on Mario's position (2.0 units) and Lakitu's camera (4.0 units), paired with whole-frame pixel verification and 40 dB audio SNR. Fixing engine C/memory undefined behavior is rewarded; changing how 1996 Mario plays scores −1.
