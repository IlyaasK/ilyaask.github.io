FPV

A first-person camera flying a heightfield, banking with your scroll velocity. Roll, dolly, and warp — no dependencies, no assets, all of it arithmetic in one canvas.

Scroll to fly

What you are inside of

A heightfield, a camera seven units above it, and a line that winds through the hills. Scroll position is distance travelled. Scroll velocity is bank angle, throttle, and the length of the streaks.

The grid runs from three units in front of the lens to four hundred and sixty out, spaced logarithmically, so the ground keeps detail as it rushes past instead of collapsing into one flat band near the horizon.

Why the horizon is the whole trick

Roll is what a body reads as flight. A camera leaning around its own forward axis drags the horizon with it, and everything else follows: yaw tracks the line, pitch stays level, and the sky gradient tilts at half the roll so it never looks painted onto the screen.

The shake is in the camera, not the page. Yaw and altitude take a small random walk proportional to throttle — the vibration you feel in your hands on a real descent.

What the last attempt got wrong

It sheared the text column sideways with a skew. Horizontal lines stayed perfectly level, so the page read as warped paper rather than motion — you cannot bank a paragraph. A camera has to be inside a scene, and text does not have an inside.

So the words stay absolutely still here, and the world moves behind them.

Where this could go

Behind the homepage as a hero, as the backdrop of a project page with a live demo, or as the whole site with the text riding in front of it. The terrain, the line, and the palette are one function each, so a different hill is one edit.
